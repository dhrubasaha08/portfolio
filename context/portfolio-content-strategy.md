# Portfolio Content Strategy

## Objective

Reposition Dhruba Saha as a **Software Engineer — Applied AI, Backend Automation & Internal Tools** while preserving open-source and embedded-systems credibility as supporting differentiation.

The portfolio is for engineering and product leaders evaluating applied-AI, backend, automation, internal-tools, integration, developer-tools, and product-oriented software roles.

## Systems Observatory direction

The visual language should feel like a polished AI control plane rather than a generic space portfolio. Use a restrained sensor-to-semantics motif: signals become structured context, decisions, and reliable system outcomes.

- Prefer deep navy/ink surfaces, high-contrast warm-white text, cool cyan/teal signal accents, and a limited amber status accent.
- Use telemetry, traces, nodes, grids, and status language as subtle supporting details rather than decoration-heavy dashboards.
- Preserve existing space or 3D elements only when they reinforce observation, navigation, or systems thinking and have lightweight fallbacks.
- Make content hierarchy, legibility, responsive behavior, keyboard access, and reduced-motion support stronger than visual novelty.

## Information architecture

1. **Hero:** name, primary headline, Germany, concise value proposition, and clear paths to selected work and contact.
2. **Current systems impact:** lead with the sanitized AI-assisted workflow and its approved result, with expert review retained.
3. **Public work:** use the ordering in `project-inventory.md`, with evidence, status, role, constraints, and outcome visible.
4. **Experience:** current sanitized role first, followed by open source, internship, and IoT leadership.
5. **Capabilities:** group applied AI, backend, automation, delivery, and embedded strengths by demonstrated application.
6. **About and photography:** connect embedded systems, infrastructure, open source, and observation to present-day systems thinking without interrupting the engineering narrative.
7. **Contact:** direct, accessible email and GitHub calls to action.

## Case-study model

Each primary case study should state:

- the problem and affected users;
- Dhruba's evidenced role and contribution;
- the system approach and important tradeoffs;
- the status: production, released OSS, team prototype, reproducible demo, or active R&D;
- the measurable or observable result;
- public evidence and disclosure boundaries.

Never use a generic project grid that gives all work equal weight or hides status and ownership.

## Content balance

- **60%:** applied AI, backend automation, internal tools, APIs, data, orchestration, and reliable delivery.
- **20%:** open source and embedded systems, led by DHT11.
- **10%:** developer tooling and active R&D, including carefully scoped Project Kyber context.
- **10%:** leadership, photography, and personal identity.

## Release standard

The first screen must communicate Dhruba's name, software-engineering identity, applied-AI/backend focus, and practical systems orientation without a technology badge wall. Every public claim must be supported by repository evidence, an approved user statement, or an explicit verification record.
